# C&I BESS Certifications: What "Certified" Really Means — and Why the Model Number Must Match

You have the power rating, the efficiency figure and the delivery date in hand. Then the grid operator, the insurer or the tender board asks one short question: *"May we see the certificate for this exact unit?"* If the folder that comes back is a stack of company-level statements plus a certificate for a model that is similar — but not the one on your order — the project stalls. Not because of the hardware. Because of the paper.

For a behind-the-meter C&I battery, a certificate is not a marketing badge. It is an evidence chain, and every link has to point at the unit you are actually buying.

## Why a vague "certified" stalls a C&I project

The pain shows up in the same place every time: the moment the documents are matched against the delivered equipment. A manufacturer says the company "is certified," or hands over one document for the whole product family. That is not what your reviewer needs. Your reviewer needs to connect three things — the standard, the certificate, the model on the nameplate. If the third link is missing, the first two do not close the case.

This is where scope matters: "certified" only means something once you can say *what* is certified, to *which* standard and *which edition*. A general assurance answers none of that.

## What a credible certificate file looks like

Below is how we read the certificate package in our own documentation — and where a manufacturer's certificate legitimately stops.

### Battery- and system-level safety: IEC 62619 and IEC 63056

MiraSol cells and battery systems are certified to IEC 62619:2022, the safety standard for secondary cells [F-CERTIFICATION-001]. MiraSol storage systems are additionally covered by IEC 63056:2020, the dedicated safety supplement for energy storage systems [F-CERTIFICATION-002].

When a buyer asks "what safety standard does this C&I BESS meet?", this pair is the starting point of the answer. Because standards are revised, the edition matters as much as the name — the document should state it [F-CERTIFICATION-001].

### Transport and market access: UN 38.3 and CE

A C&I system travels before it operates, so the file also needs the logistics documents. Lithium-battery transport meets UN 38.3 requirements [F-CERTIFICATION-004]. For the European market, the CE mark is self-declared in accordance with EN IEC 62619 / EN IEC 63056 [F-CERTIFICATION-003]. Together, this package — CE, IEC 62619, IEC 63056 and UN 38.3 — covers the common market-access requirements of MiraSol's main overseas markets, EN and DE [F-CERTIFICATION-006].

### The boundary: grid connection is project-level

One item will not be in a manufacturer's certificate file, and that is expected. Grid codes — for example the German VDE-AR-N 4105 for a *Gewerbespeicher* — are local, project-level compliance that is met on the integrator side. The vendor does not claim specific grid-connection certificates [F-CERTIFICATION-005]. Budget for this in the project plan and the connection process, not in the factory folder.

### Matching the paper to the unit: the model number

A certificate is scoped to the certified object — for MiraSol, cells/battery systems under IEC 62619 [F-CERTIFICATION-001] and storage systems under the ESS supplement IEC 63056 [F-CERTIFICATION-002]. The decisive check is therefore a reconciliation, not a glance at the logo. Does the model/type on the certificate and its annexes match the nameplate of the unit on your order? If it names a different variant — or none at all — it does not evidence the unit you are buying. Ask for the document that names your model before filing the pack.

## A five-point checklist before you file the certificate pack

| # | Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The standard name **and edition** are stated (e.g. IEC 62619:2022) | The edition identifies the standard version the certificate covers [F-CERTIFICATION-001]. |
| 2 | The scope is explicit: cell/battery safety vs. ESS supplement | Confirms whether the document covers cell/battery safety (IEC 62619) or the ESS supplement (IEC 63056) [F-CERTIFICATION-001][F-CERTIFICATION-002]. |
| 3 | The model/type on the certificate matches the nameplate of the ordered unit | The ordered unit must fall within the scope the certificate covers [F-CERTIFICATION-001][F-CERTIFICATION-002]. |
| 4 | Transport and CE documents are present | UN 38.3 covers lithium-battery transport; CE is self-declared to EN IEC 62619/63056 [F-CERTIFICATION-004][F-CERTIFICATION-003]. |
| 5 | Grid connection is planned as a separate, local step | Grid codes such as VDE-AR-N 4105 are integrator-side, project-level compliance [F-CERTIFICATION-005]. |
